# Formalism Comparison — SHACL vs. SysML v2 for Typed Standards

*A synthesis of two companion formalization framings of the Typed Standards Specification (v0.1.x).*

**Companion to:** [`typed-standards-specification.md`](../docs/architecture/typed-standards-specification.md) (normative for package shape) and [`open-questions.md`](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md) (the open-questions registry). This document is **descriptive and exploratory**, not normative: it compares two ways of re-expressing the spec, neither of which is (yet) a committed project artifact. The two framings it synthesizes are the **SHACL formalization** (instance-data shapes) and the **SysML v2 formalization** (system structure, behavior, lifecycle, and requirements traceability), both derived from the same consolidated formal model of the spec.

---

## 1. Thesis — complementary, not competing

The two formalisms are **largely complementary**, and the boundary between them is precise. They are not two candidate encodings of the same thing; they encode different *aspects* of the spec, and the spec has at least four aspects at once:

- a **data schema** — the cryptographic envelope, the evidence package, the typed-claims layer, the attestation payloads;
- a **behavior specification** — the §9.2 ordered verification check list, the publisher pipeline;
- a **set of lifecycle state machines** — publication / withdrawal / reinstatement / supersession / revision succession; the ADR-0020 §B signing-status axis (`unsigned` → `signed`, orthogonal to visibility — an unsigned node reaches neither `sealed` nor `public`); trust-registry key activation / deprecation / revocation; notebook provenance;
- a **normative-requirements corpus** — the MUST / SHOULD / MAY clauses of §5.1, §8.x, and §9.

The division of labor is:

> **SHACL is authoritative for instance-data conformance/validation at the envelope and typed-claims layers** — does *this concrete signed node / claim document* satisfy the graph-shape rules (presence, datatype, cardinality, value-set membership, conditional presence, intra-graph reference resolution, uniqueness)?
>
> **SysML v2 is authoritative for system structure, behavior, lifecycle state machines, and requirements traceability across the whole architecture** — what *are* the types and their specialization relations, in what *order* does the verifier run its checks, what *states* can a node move between and on what *triggers*, and *which* structural element and *which* check discharge a given MUST?

Crucially, the two formalisms meet at a shared hard boundary (§3): **neither can perform the cryptographic verification itself.** Signature math, hash/canonicalization recomputation, RFC 3161 cert-chain validation, and RFC 6962 Rekor inclusion stay out-of-band in a real verifier. SHACL declares them out of scope (`expressibleInShacl=false`); SysML models them as `action def`s with opaque bodies *delegated to external system parts*. Each formalism marks the same line — they agree on where formalization stops and a running verifier begins.

The relationship is therefore not a contest to pick a winner. SHACL is the *executable validator* of instance documents; SysML is the *map* of the structure, procedure, lifecycle, and obligations that surround those documents. Where they overlap — both record the closed enums `ts:direction ∈ {Increase, Decrease, NoSignificantChange}` and `ts:relation ∈ {GreaterThan, LessThan, ApproximatelyEqual}` — they record the *same* closure for different purposes: SHACL constrains the **instance value** (and rejects a bad one); SysML constrains the **type** (and documents the closure as a model element a requirement can cite). They are two views of one model, not two competing models.

The middle layers (taxonomy, profiles, cross-host publication, the §9.2 check list) are genuinely shared — SHACL validates the instances, SysML models the structure and behavior around them.

## 3. The hard boundary both formalisms share

Both formalisms stop at the same wall, and it is worth stating without hedging: **neither SHACL nor SysML v2 can perform the cryptographic verification, hash recomputation, or transparency-log inclusion checks.** Those remain out-of-band, performed by a real verifier (today, `@typedstandards/verify-core`; since [ADR-0021](../docs/adr/0021-produce-core-extraction.md) the producer side shares the same canonicalization/hash chain by construction, `@typedstandards/produce-core` depending on `verify-core`). Specifically, the following §9.2 checks are outside *both* formalisms:

- **#1 Envelope integrity** — recompute the envelope hash via RFC 8785 JCS + SHA-256 and compare. Requires canonicalization + hashing.
- **#2 Signature mathematics** — verify the Ed25519ph signature over the envelope-hash hex string. Requires elliptic-curve cryptography.
- **#4 Content hash verification** — recompute the multihash over the off-log content under the resolved canonicalization rule.
- **#5 / #14 Trust-registry verdict and signer↔kid cross-check** — a data join against a fetched (or inlined) `.well-known` trust registry + temporal status comparison. The intra-package half of #14 is graph-checkable; the registry lookup is not.
- **#7 Timestamp validity** — verify the RFC 3161 token signature *and* validate the embedded ECDSA-P384 cert chain to the pinned FreeTSA RSA-4096 root. Full X.509 chain validation.
- **#8 Transparency-log inclusion** — verify RFC 6962 Merkle inclusion against the pinned Rekor P-256 log key + signed checkpoint.
- **#9 BlobRef integrity** — HTTPS fetch + SHA-256 recomputation + size confirmation.

How each formalism marks the line is the tell that they agree on *where* it falls:

- **SHACL** flags these `expressibleInShacl=false` and simply omits them — they have no positive representation in a shapes graph. A SHACL constraint is graph-shape validation only (presence, datatype, cardinality, value-set membership, conditional presence, intra-graph reference resolution / string equality on materialized values). Anything needing crypto, a hash recomputation, a network fetch, or a cross-graph trust-registry resolution is out.
- **SysML** *does* give them a positive representation — as `action def`s (`CheckSignature`, `CheckTimestamp`, `CheckRekorInclusion`, `CheckBlobRefs`, `computeEnvelopeHash` as a `calc`) — but every such body is **opaque** and **delegated to an external system `part def`** (the TSA, the Rekor log, the publisher's trust registry, a blob store). SysML maps the procedure and names the external systems it calls; it is honest that it is not the verifier. The envelope-hash derivation is a `calc` whose body delegates to the crypto subsystem precisely because SysML evaluates expressions but does not implement SHA-256 or JCS.

The consequence for anyone reading either formalization: **a green SHACL report and a fully-traced SysML model together still do not constitute verification.** They establish that a document is well-shaped and that the procedure around it is well-defined. The cryptographic trust property is supplied only by running the out-of-band checks in a conformant verifier against public infrastructure. Both formalisms are explicit about this so that neither is mistaken for the thing it models.

## 4. Relationship to the open-questions registry

Formalization is a pressure test: it forces decisions the prose left implicit, and several of those decisions land squarely on open questions already tracked in [`open-questions.md`](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md). The two formalisms touch the registry on different axes — SHACL is **vocabulary/ontology-adjacent**, SysML is **lifecycle/system-adjacent** — which is itself evidence of their complementarity.

### 4.1 SHACL-adjacent open questions

- **[Q10](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q10--civic-claim-vocabulary-as-a-full-ontology) — Civic Claim Vocabulary as a full ontology.** Promoted to issue 005, with a 2026-07-01 reopen flag (the promotion stands only if scoping names a concrete adopter- or reasoner-driven need for OWL semantics); the registry entry now also records this bundle's own ontology draft (`ontology/typedClaims.ttl`) as the candidate artifact — see the §7 postscript. This is the most directly SHACL-adjacent question. The typed-claims layer's conformance (§8.11.3) is defined as validation against "the SHACL shapes published with the Typed Standards Claim Vocabulary." Whether `ts:` is a controlled vocabulary or a full OWL ontology changes *what those shapes can assume* — OWL axioms, reasoner-compatible class definitions, and external alignment (PROV-O / SDMX / Data Cube / Schema.org) all alter how a SHACL formalization fixes class semantics and what it can defer to a reasoner. A committed SHACL artifact would need this resolved (or would itself help resolve it).
- **[Q41](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q41--ontology-assembly-strategy-core-vs-natural-extension-vs-institutional-adapter) — Ontology assembly strategy (core vs. natural-extension vs. institutional-adapter).** Whether `ts:TrendClaim` etc. and the QEC sub-ontology are first-class TS vocabulary or SKOS-mapped subsets of mature ontologies directly determines the *targetClass* structure of the SHACL shapes and whether validation leans on `owl:sameAs` / `skos:exactMatch` alignment edges. The SHACL framing's vocabulary-selection rule (TC-R14: prefer the most widely adopted external vocabulary; include `owl:sameAs` / `skos:exactMatch` for less-common choices) is exactly the seam Q41 governs. The Q41 entry now additionally carries recorded (unresolved) positions from a formalization-review meeting on how SHACL should be framed at all — see the §7 postscript.
- **[Q14](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q14--geographic-and-temporal-scope-nullability) — Geographic/temporal scope nullability.** The SHACL framing models `ts:scope`'s required geographic + temporal components as Q14-contingent `sh:Warning` rather than hard `sh:Violation`, anticipating that v1.0 may relax "required" to "present unless explicitly waived." Q14 also covers civic-coded subtype names (`ts:PolicePrecinct`), which the SHACL shapes' `sh:in` / class taxonomy would carry.
- **[Q5](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q5--claimsjsonld-and-upstream-evidencejson-implementation-timing) — typed-claim build-out timing.** The entire typed-claims layer is `specified, not built`; the SHACL shapes for `content/claim/v1` are the most-developed sub-area of the formalization but validate a layer no adopter yet emits. A committed SHACL artifact is Xanadu-gated on the same trigger as Q5 (a real typed-content producer).
- **[Q37](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q37--type-registry-mechanism-and-governance-for-the-content-and-attestation-namespaces) — Type-registry mechanism and governance.** SHACL shapes target a set of sub-type URIs (`sh:in` over the `type` field). Q37 leaves *how* sub-type URIs are registered/versioned deliberately unspecified (Xanadu-gated). A SHACL formalization that hard-codes the v0.1 sub-type set is correct for v0.1 but would need a governance mechanism to stay current — the same gap Q37 holds open.

### 4.2 SysML-adjacent open questions

- **[Q49](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q49--lifecyclevisibility-model-vs-a-vcs-native-git-versioned-evidence-notebook-workflow) — Lifecycle/visibility model vs. a VCS-native workflow.** Resolved 2026-06-15 by [ADR-0016](../docs/adr/0016-vcs-native-lifecycle-mapping.md), this is the most directly SysML-adjacent question. Its resolution *adds state and structure the SysML model would carry*: (A) the `visibility` field's values become `sealed`/`public`, with visibility / lifecycle-status / host-display named as **three orthogonal dimensions** — exactly the kind of orthogonal-axis decomposition SysML's parallel `state def`s express (the SysML framing already runs `ContentNodeVisibility` ∥ `ContentNodeLifecycleStatus` as parallel machines, alongside the `SigningStatus` and `ContentNodeLocation` axes); (C) mints a new `attestation/revises/v1` sub-type distinct from `attestation/supersedes/v1`, which is a new specialization of `AttestationNode` and a new lifecycle transition. All of this has since landed in the spec text itself (the 2026-08-03 v0.1.4 revision: §8.10.5 lineage, §8.10.6 orthogonal dimensions, the 16-row §8.12.1 table, plus the `vcsRef` envelope field per ADR-0016 §B), so the SysML framing now models ratified spec rather than a pending ADR. A SysML model is the natural place to keep the three-axis decomposition and the revises-vs-supersedes distinction legible.
- **[Q20](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q20--visibility-lifecycle-and-attestpublish-semantics) — Visibility lifecycle and attest/publish semantics.** Resolved by [ADR-0010](../docs/adr/0010-visibility-lifecycle-location-attestations.md): visibility is the *structural consequence* of which `attestation/*` nodes reference a content node, and multi-cycle withdrawal/reinstatement has no cycle limit. This is precisely what the SysML `ContentNodeVisibility` and `ContentNodeLifecycleStatus` state machines encode (transitions accept attestation events; multi-cycle re-uses the same transitions with no counter). The `attestation/publishes/v1` delegated-publisher branch (Q20's residual) is the `isDelegatedPublisher` guard the SysML model leaves as a body-less `calc` because the predicate is "not yet formalizable beyond permitted in principle."
- **[Q3](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q3--first-non-github-identity-provider) — First non-GitHub identity provider.** The `signer.bindingTier` ladder is informative ("only the GitHub tier is built"). The SysML framing models `BindingTier` as an **extensible** enum using the spec's actual registry value (`platform`) rather than freezing a closed set — and explicitly declines to formalize the unimplemented `orcid` / `did:web` / `notarized` per-tier identifier schemas. Q3's resolution (which provider lands next) is what would promote one of those tiers from informative to modeled.
- **[Q4](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q4--trace-capture) — Trace capture.** The five-span-kind set is normalized as conformant only for v0.1; SysML would model `SpanKind` as a provisional enum with no span field schema beyond the kind enumeration (the spec gives none). Q4's resolution (real OTel SDK vs. Agent Receipts) revises §8.4 and the trace structure both formalisms carry.

### 4.3 Shared-adjacent: conformance

- **[Q16](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q16--formal-conformance-criteria) — Formal conformance criteria.** Open, and the one question *both* formalisms bear on directly. Q16 needs "a versioned conformance suite, a reference corpus, and a conformance-claims registration mechanism." The SHACL shapes are a candidate machine-checkable *instance-conformance* corpus; the SysML `requirement def` tree with `satisfy`/`verify` edges is a candidate *requirements-conformance* map (which MUST is discharged by which structure/behavior). Neither *is* the conformance suite Q16 asks for, but each is a partial draft of one half of it. The [Q15](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q15--external-verification-testing) offline-bundle harness already surfaced the conformance *shape* (the §9.2 check-list + pinned anchors + the §8.8 bundle serialization); a formalization is the natural next step toward the *formalization* Q16 still lacks. A 2026-07-22 sharpening of Q16 is worth registering here, because it lands squarely on the §3 boundary: for an independent implementation the highest-risk conformance surface is **RFC 8785 JCS parity** (ECMAScript number-to-string formatting, UTF-16 code-unit key ordering, escaping), not the signature layer — and conformance vectors should pin canonical *bytes* for the awkward cases. That surface is out-of-band for *both* formalisms (canonicalization is exactly what neither can execute), so the sharpened Q16 confirms that the highest-value part of a future conformance suite is the part no formalization in this bundle can supply.

## 5. Recommendation — both exploratory, each with a concrete adoption trigger

Per the project's [xanadu-doctrine](../docs/architecture/xanadu-doctrine.md), a formalism should **not** be promoted to a committed, maintained artifact without a real consumer that needs it. "It would be cleaner to have a SHACL/SysML model" is explicitly *not* a sufficient trigger; "it would be more rigorous" is the foundational-layer version of the over-design the doctrine exists to prevent (the same reasoning [Q37](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q37--type-registry-mechanism-and-governance-for-the-content-and-attestation-namespaces) applies to the type-registry mechanism). Accordingly, both framings are recommended to stay **exploratory** for now: valuable as a pressure test (they surfaced the spec ambiguities in §6 below and sharpened several open questions in §4), kept as study documents, **not** wired into CI, the publish route, or the verifier, and **not** cited as normative.

Each has a distinct concrete trigger under which promotion to a committed artifact would become Xanadu-justified:

**Promote the SHACL formalization when a third party needs machine-checkable conformance of published packages.** The concrete trigger: an external implementer (datHere, an academic partner, a second registry operator) commits to consume or emit packages and needs to *validate* them against the standard without trusting a prose reading — i.e., the moment [Q16](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q16--formal-conformance-criteria) gets a real external consumer, or the moment [Q5](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q5--claimsjsonld-and-upstream-evidencejson-implementation-timing)'s first typed-content producer needs the `content/claim/v1` shapes to validate their emissions. At that point the SHACL shapes stop being a study and become the published validator the spec already references by name (§8.11.3), and their maintenance is justified by a consumer who is blocked without them. Until then, the shapes are a faithful but un-needed encoding of a layer (typed claims) no one emits yet. The [Q10](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q10--civic-claim-vocabulary-as-a-full-ontology) / [Q41](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q41--ontology-assembly-strategy-core-vs-natural-extension-vs-institutional-adapter) ontology decisions should land *before or with* a committed SHACL artifact, since they fix the class semantics the shapes assume.

> *Trigger status, 2026-08-18: not fired.* The producer-side context has strengthened since this trigger was written — `@typedstandards/produce-core` shipped beside `verify-core` ([ADR-0021](../docs/adr/0021-produce-core-extraction.md); [Q59](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q59--producer-side-core-no-portable-counterpart-to-verify-core) resolved 2026-08-01) and this hub repo now publishes `@typedstandards/civic-typed-harness` as its first consumer ([ADR-0022](../docs/adr/0022-civic-typed-harness-packaging.md)), which materially lowers the cost of the second producer the trigger contemplates. But no external implementer has committed to consume or emit packages, and the typed-claims layer still has no emitter ([Q5](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q5--claimsjsonld-and-upstream-evidencejson-implementation-timing) remains open) — so the shapes stay a study artifact.

**Promote the SysML v2 formalization when the verification/lifecycle system needs a tool-supported model.** The concrete trigger: the verifier and lifecycle machinery grow complex enough — or gain enough independent implementations — that a prose-plus-code description stops being sufficient to reason about correctness, and an engineering audience needs a tool-checkable model of the procedure, the state machines, and the requirements traceability. Plausible specific triggers: an institutional/engineering adopter that works in MBSE tooling (the reviewer framing behind [Q42](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q42--methodological-soundness-layer-does-the-method-support-the-kind-of-judgment)–[Q44](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q44--audience-stratified-access-audit--epistemic--computational-replay) explicitly contrasts claims-as-requirements with claims-as-hypotheses in SysMLv2 / OpenMBEE terms); a second independent verifier implementation that needs the §9.2 procedure and the lifecycle state machines specified tool-checkably rather than read off the reference code; or [Q49](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q49--lifecyclevisibility-model-vs-a-vcs-native-git-versioned-evidence-notebook-workflow)'s three-orthogonal-axis lifecycle model proving hard to keep coherent in prose as `attestation/revises/v1` and the `vcsRef` field land. Until one of those arrives, the SysML model is a high-value map with no navigator who needs it.

> *Trigger status, 2026-08-18: not fired.* The third plausible trigger's precondition has now materialized — `attestation/revises/v1` and `vcsRef` landed in the spec's 2026-08-03 v0.1.4 revision — but the prose (§8.10.5–§8.10.6) has so far carried the three-axis decomposition coherently, so the condition ("proving hard to keep coherent in prose") is being tested in real time, not met. No MBSE-tooling adopter and no second independent verifier implementation has arrived.

Two cross-cutting notes on the recommendation:

1. **They are not mutually exclusive, and adopting one does not imply the other.** Because they cover different aspects (instance validation vs. structure/behavior/requirements), a future where the SHACL shapes are committed (a third-party validator exists) while the SysML model stays exploratory (no MBSE adopter yet) is coherent — and is in fact the more likely near-term shape, since the typed-claims validator has a clearer line of sight to a real consumer than the MBSE model does.
2. **Whichever is promoted, the §3 boundary must be carried forward verbatim.** A committed SHACL artifact must keep its `expressibleInShacl=false` exclusions explicit; a committed SysML model must keep its crypto/log/network `action def`s opaque-and-delegated. The single most important property of both framings is that they are honest about where formalization stops and a running verifier begins — promoting either without that honesty would mis-sell a well-shaped document as a verified one.

## 6. Appendix — spec ambiguities surfaced by the dual formalization

Formalizing the spec twice forced decisions the prose left implicit. These are recorded as **ambiguities to resolve against the reference implementation or a future ADR**, not as model choices that override the spec. Both framings independently hit most of them, which is a useful signal that they are genuine gaps rather than artifacts of one notation. Two of the eight (items 1 and 3) have since been resolved by the spec's 2026-08-03 v0.1.4 reconciliation revision — the pressure test worked exactly as intended — and the resolutions are recorded in place below:

1. **`contentProfile` placement.** As of the bundle's first release, §8.1.1 listed `contentProfile` as a *top-level* field while §8.2 / §8.7 referenced `metadata.contentProfile`. The SHACL framing initially rooted it at top level; the SysML framing placed it on `Metadata` (the more-cited access path) and flagged the divergence. The two formalizations *resolved it differently*, which was the clearest possible evidence the spec had to pin one home before either model could be authoritative. **Resolved 2026-08-03**: the spec's v0.1.4 revision pins **`metadata.contentProfile`** (§8.1.2 placement note — "spec-text alignment, no wire change"; `producerProfile` stays top-level), with the alignment note crediting the formal model's validation pass for documenting the shipped wire correctly. Both framings now model the ratified placement, and the consistency invariant reads `metadata.contentProfile == "datHere"` iff `producerProfile` starts `ai-assisted-analysis/datHere`.
2. **`output` / `trace` / `skillText` string-or-BlobRef union.** Both formalisms lack a clean union type; both used paired nullable carriers (SysML: `output` + `outputBlob`) or a documented `sh:or`, with an implied exclusivity. The underlying choice is genuinely a union; a future schema should say which carrier is present.
3. **`attestation/locatedAt/v1` payload fingerprint-field name collision** — independently registered as [Q48](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q48--attestationlocatedatv1-payload-contenthash-name-collision-with-the-structural-primitive). The §8.12.1 table originally named the payload's target-fingerprint field `contentHash`, colliding with the structural primitive's own `contentHash` (the node's own off-log fingerprint), while the reference implementation emitted `targetContentHash`. **Resolved 2026-08-03**: the v0.1.4 revision ratifies **`targetContentHash`** in the §8.12.1 `locatedAt` row and §8.10.2, following the same disambiguation precedent as `targetNodeId`; no wire change (the implementation already emitted the ratified name). The formalizations' `locatedAt` shapes carry the ratified name — the divergence is closed.
4. **`(unspecified)` attestation payload fields.** `scope`, `availability`, `certificationScheme`, `validityWindow`, `methodology`, `scoringRubric`, `results` are named without datatypes. Both framings carry them as `String` placeholders / un-constrained nodes with an explicit "(unspecified)" marker — neither invents a type.
5. **`Magnitude` / `Component`** left abstract with no fields per the spec's explicit "do NOT invent fields." An empty `item def` / a property-less node shape is the faithful encoding of "referenced but not defined."
6. **Delegated-publisher predicate ([Q20](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q20--visibility-lifecycle-and-attestpublish-semantics)).** The `isDelegatedPublisher` guard / "signer-matched" predicate is identity-equality today but must widen once delegated-publisher mechanics land. The SysML `calc def` is body-less at exactly this seam; a SHACL shape can check the identity-equality half but not the delegated half.
7. **`bindingTier` vocabulary.** The §8.5 ladder is informative; the §8.3.3 registry example carries `platform`. Both framings declined to freeze a closed enum the spec does not freeze — the SHACL framing demoted the closed `sh:in` to a non-violating `sh:Info` ladder hint (whose value set carries the ladder tokens the §6.2 glossary's `signer` entry enumerates per the §8.5 graded ladder, `oauth` included, plus the registry-attested `platform` / `legacy_embedded`); the SysML framing marked `BindingTier` extensible. A related annotation moved the other way: an earlier adversarial-review pass removed a `claude-code-self-report` "deprecated 2026-04-28" annotation as unsupported, but the spec now states exactly that (§8.6 marks the value legacy, deprecated as of 2026-04-28, retained in the vocabulary), so the SysML framing's 2026-08-18 pass restored the annotation with the spec citation.
8. **captureMethod literals as strings, not enum members.** The three v0.1 values are hyphenated (`chat-flow-stream`); the SysML framing carries them as `String` constants (KerML identifiers cannot contain hyphens), and the SHACL framing as `sh:in` string literals. Modeling them as enum identifiers would silently rename them to values the spec never defines.

## 7. Postscript (2026-08-18) — formalization-review positions now on record

Since this document's first release, the open-questions registry has come to carry **recorded positions** from a formalization-review meeting between the maintainer and the formalization collaborator (meeting 2026-07-23; registered 2026-08-03). They are positions on record, not resolutions — the registry entries say so explicitly — but they bear directly on this document's thesis and recommendation, so they are noted here:

- **[Q41](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q41--ontology-assembly-strategy-core-vs-natural-extension-vs-institutional-adapter) records a reframing of what SHACL is *for*.** The formalization collaborator's position: SHACL's more interesting use is **shape-specification** — declaring the classes of entities in the spec's universe and which parts of an arbitrary RDF graph are admissible, serving as a *gate for data ingestion* (with feedback) and a *reference point for transformation output* — rather than "verification"; validity in the correct-processing / tamper-evidence sense stays outside SHACL's scope. In the bundle's logical-components mapping, **the spec's data schema maps to SHACL; everything else maps to SysML** — which is this document's §1 division of labor stated as a position, and the shape-specification framing is consistent with the §3 boundary (neither formalism performs the cryptographic verification). The entry also records the maintainer's decision to **punt the SHACL step in the verifier core** (keep the output shape JSON; translate JSON → JSON-LD at the point SHACL validation is needed) and affirms an interim use: SHACL as spec self-consistency checking even with a single user. Q41 notes that this document's §5 promotion triggers **stand as the SHACL-path record** — the reframing shifts what a promoted SHACL artifact would be *called* (an ingestion gate rather than a validator headline), not the Xanadu gate on promoting it.
- **[Q10](../docs/architecture/open-questions.md#q10--civic-claim-vocabulary-as-a-full-ontology) records this bundle's ontology draft as the candidate artifact.** The bundle's `ontology/typedClaims.ttl` — previously an orphan (unreferenced) within the bundle, noted as acceptable for now — is registered as the existing candidate artifact for the CCV-to-ontology promotion, **not** promoted. The maintainer's recorded intent is to **adopt it after a further round of review**, with the stated goal of a *minimum ontology extensible to different domains* (designed from existing ontologies wherever possible, extended only minimally). A namespace-coordination fact is on record for that adoption round to reconcile (the draft's `https://typedstandards.org/ns/ts#` vs. the shipped harness's `urn:civic-evidence:` identifiers and `…/ns/evidence/` vocabulary URI), and a secondhand suggestion that the spec not *mandate* QEC is registered without being adopted.

Neither entry changes the §5 recommendation: both framings remain exploratory, and the recorded positions align with — rather than override — the promotion triggers above. What has changed is that the triggers now have an institutional home: the registry cites this document as the SHACL-path record, and the ontology half of the bundle has a named, Q10-gated adoption path.
